Compliance & regulatory framework

How Wallet Partners LLC intends to operate within card-network rules, Dominican financial regulation and the oversight of a sponsoring bank. This page summarises the programme; full policies are available to prospective sponsors under NDA.

Last updated 2026-09-08

Status. Wallet Partners is pre-launch. The controls below describe the programme we are building to the standard a sponsoring bank will require. Items marked as "at launch" will be validated before the first live transaction.

1. Regulatory map

Dominican Republic

  • Law No. 183-02, Monetary and Financial Law, and the Monetary Board's Payment Systems Regulation (SIPARD), most recently amended in August 2025. The Banco Central de la República Dominicana oversees the payment system; card acquiring is performed by or under licensed financial intermediaries supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos. Wallet Partners will operate as a program manager / payment facilitator under a licensed member bank and will register or license any locally required entity.
  • Law No. 155-17 against money laundering and terrorist financing, its regulations and the guidance of the Unidad de Análisis Financiero (UAF): merchant due diligence, beneficial-ownership identification, transaction monitoring, record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting through the sponsoring bank.
  • Law No. 172-13 on protection of personal data: lawful processing, security measures and data-subject rights for merchants, their owners and cardholders.
  • Law No. 358-05 on consumer protection (Pro Consumidor): transparent pricing, receipts and complaint handling at the point of sale.
  • Tax. Dirección General de Impuestos Internos (DGII) rules on card-transaction withholding (Norma General 08-04 and successors) and the fuel-specific tax regime (Laws 112-00 and 557-05, under which retail fuel is subject to selective and ad-valorem taxes rather than ITBIS). Withholding parameters for fuel merchants will be confirmed with the sponsoring bank and tax counsel before launch.
  • Fuel sector. Only stations holding a valid operating resolution from the Ministry of Industry, Commerce and MSMEs (MICM) are eligible; the resolution and the station's DGII registration form part of every merchant file.

United States and international

  • Bank Secrecy Act / AML programme aligned with the sponsoring bank's requirements and, where applicable, FinCEN registration; OFAC and other sanctions screening of all merchants and beneficial owners.
  • Card-network rules (Visa Core Rules and Mastercard Rules) for payment facilitators and sponsored merchants, including merchant category codes 5541 (service stations) and 5542 (automated fuel dispensers), automated-fuel-dispenser authorisation and completion rules, and the MATCH / terminated-merchant checks.
  • PCI DSS v4.0 as a Level 1 service provider at launch, using point-to-point-encrypted (P2PE) terminals so that no cardholder data touches Wallet Partners' systems in clear text.

2. Merchant onboarding (KYB / KYC)

  • Legal-entity verification: constitutive documents, RNC, commercial registry, MICM operating resolution, proof of address and site photographs.
  • Identification of directors and every beneficial owner at 10% or more; identity documents; PEP and sanctions screening; adverse-media review.
  • Financial profile: expected monthly volume, average ticket, card-present share; bank account ownership verification for settlement.
  • Risk rating and approval workflow: Wallet Partners prepares and recommends; the sponsoring bank approves, conditions or declines. Periodic refresh at least every two years, or on trigger events.

3. Transaction monitoring and fraud

  • Rules for fuel: per-card and per-pump velocity limits, ticket-size caps consistent with tank capacity, duplicate-transaction detection, unusual night-time patterns, and card-testing detection.
  • Portfolio monitoring against network chargeback and fraud thresholds; early-warning reporting to the bank.
  • Escalation of unusual activity to the bank's compliance function; suspicious-transaction reporting through the bank to the UAF and, where applicable, FinCEN.

4. Funds, settlement and reserves

  • Network settlement is received by the sponsoring bank into a program account it controls. Wallet Partners does not hold merchant funds outside that structure.
  • Next-business-day settlement to merchants in Dominican pesos, with reserves and delayed-release rules set with the bank per merchant risk tier.
  • Daily reconciliation of network settlement, merchant payouts, fees and reserves; monthly reporting to the bank.

5. Information security

  • P2PE-validated terminals; tokenisation for any stored references; no storage of PAN, track or CVV data.
  • Segmented cloud environment, encryption at rest, key management with dual control, MFA for all administrative access, centralised logging and alerting.
  • Annual penetration testing, vulnerability management, vendor risk assessments and an incident-response plan with notification timelines agreed with the bank and, where required, under Law 172-13.
  • Coordinated vulnerability disclosure via security.txt.

6. Governance

  • Designated compliance officer; board-approved AML, sanctions, privacy and information-security policies; annual independent review.
  • Training for all staff and field technicians on AML red flags, data protection and card-network rules.
  • Complete records retained for at least five years (ten where Law 155-17 requires), available to the sponsoring bank and regulators on request; contractual right-to-audit for the bank.
  • Merchant complaints handled within the terms agreed with the bank and Pro Consumidor rules; escalation path documented on every receipt and statement.

7. Document list available to sponsors

  • AML/CFT programme and risk assessment
  • Sanctions screening procedure
  • Merchant underwriting policy and file template
  • Transaction-monitoring rule set (fuel)
  • Information-security policy set and PCI DSS plan
  • Privacy programme (Law 172-13)
  • Business-continuity and incident-response plans
  • Draft sponsorship term sheet and reserve model
